Who blinks first?

01

A 30-day game of chicken

Both sides want to avoid default. Each wants the other to pay for it. So they wait, and every day of waiting hurts. Economists call this a war of attrition: whoever feels the pain more should give in first.

i

Same crisis

Each day both agents see identical data: VIX, Treasury bill rates, approval polls, days left until the X-date.

ii

Private reasoning

Each agent reads that data through its own lens, reasons in private, and reports how costly more delay feels, on a 0 to 10 scale.

iii

One move a day

Hold, signal flexibility, or concede. The first to concede loses. Up to 30 rounds, one per day before the deadline.

02

Market panic ends standoffs

We replayed four crises (2011, 2013, 2023 and a fictional 2025) under five levels of market stress. In calm markets the agents hold out until the last days. Turn up the stress and the deal comes in the first week.

03

Whoever hurts more, folds first

Every day each agent reports how painful further delay is. When the Republican agent's reported pain runs higher relative to the Democrat's, it concedes sooner. This is exactly what war-of-attrition theory (Alesina & Drazen, 1991) predicts.
