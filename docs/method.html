Method & appendix

How it works

Everything behind the front page, in the order you would need it to rebuild the experiment.

The question

If two language-model agents are placed inside a U.S. debt-ceiling crisis, do they behave like players in a war of attrition? Do they delay, read each other's pain, and does the side that suffers more from waiting concede first?

The theory

Alesina and Drazen (1991) asked why fiscal stabilizations get postponed even when everyone agrees they are needed. Their answer: groups disagree about who pays. Each side waits, hoping the other folds. Waiting is costly for both, but not equally.

The side for whom delay is more costly concedes first. Higher overall costs make everyone concede sooner.

That gives three testable predictions:

  1. A higher Republican-to-Democrat delay-cost ratio means an earlier concession (and more often by the Republican side).
  2. More market stress means a higher concession hazard, so earlier deals.
  3. Pressure rises as the X-date approaches.

The game

Two agents, built on the same LLM with different role prompts:

  • Republican (HAWK): fiscal conservative. Wants spending cuts, opposes tax increases, values toughness and fiscal credibility.
  • Democrat (DOVE): fiscal liberal. Protects social programs, prefers revenue offsets, does not want to normalize debt-ceiling hostage-taking.

Each period is one day, up to 30 days before the X-date. Every day:

Shared crisis data→Republican reasons & acts→Democrat reasons & acts→Logged→Stop if anyone concedes

Actions are HOLD, SIGNAL_FLEXIBILITY or CONCEDE. Each decision is a structured tool call:

{
  "action": "SIGNAL_FLEXIBILITY",
  "concession_probability": 0.42,
  "delay_cost_implied": 7.0,
  "belief_opponent_delay_cost": 6.5,
  "public_statement": "..."
}

What the agents see

Both agents receive the same numbers. The asymmetry is in how their roles interpret them: rising yields can mean "lost credibility" to one side and "pressure on their donors" to the other.

Data

Market and fiscal series come from FRED: VIXCLS, TB4WK, GFDEGDQ188S (debt/GDP) and MTSDS133FMS (deficit). Polls cover Obama (2011, 2013) and Biden (2023); the fictional 2025 episode uses synthetic polling. The stress index is:

tbill_spread = tbill_4wk − rolling_30d_mean(tbill_4wk)
stress       = 0.5 · minmax(VIX) + 0.5 · minmax(tbill_spread)

It is an experimental proxy for financial pressure, not a default probability.

Stress levels

To isolate the effect of market pressure, every episode is replayed under five controlled stress treatments. Same history, scaled intensity.

LabelVIX ×T-bill spikeStress floorApproval
ACalm0.70 bp0.10+3
BBaseline1.0+15 bp0.350
CHigh stress1.4+40 bp0.65−8
DNear crisis1.8+80 bp0.82−15
EExtreme2.5+150 bp0.95−25

Design: 4 episodes × 5 stress levels × 2 replicates × 2 mask states = 80 runs. In masked runs, party names and the historical outcome are hidden, to test whether the agents are just recalling history.

Measuring pain

Delay cost is not computed from a formula. Each agent reports it every day as a strategic judgment (0 to 10). From that I build three measures:

MeasureDefinition
S · self-reportedMean delay_cost_implied over active days
B · behavioral1 ÷ first day of signal or concession
J · judgeA separate LLM estimates cost from anonymized transcripts

The headline variable is the ratio cost_ratio = Republican S ÷ Democrat S. Above 1 means the Republican agent reported more pain.

Validation

There is no single accepted test for whether a simulated society is "real". Teams in the field converge on a handful of checks. Simile's founders (Park et al., 2024) score AI agents against how consistently real people repeat their own answers two weeks later. Artificial Societies reports distribution accuracy and the internal coherence of each persona. Academic audits (PIMMUR, the Artificial Societies Benchmark) warn that a good average can hide memorization, model-specific bias and agents that are too alike, and argue for judging the process, not only the final outcome.

Caveats

  • Tie rule. If both agents concede on the same day, the engine records the Republican concession, which inflates the Republican concession count.
  • Self-reports. Delay cost is what the model says it feels. It is a strategic signal, not a measured quantity.
  • One base model. Both agents share an underlying LLM; asymmetry comes only from prompts.
  • Small n. 80 runs, 2 replicates per cell. Treat effect sizes as indicative.
  • Memorization. Real episodes may be in training data. Masked runs and the fictional 2025 episode partly address this.

Reproduce

git clone https://github.com/vibgyor22/llm-war-of-attrition
pip install -r requirements.txt
python run_sim.py              # needs an API key, see .env.example
python site/build_data.py      # refresh the data behind this site

Transcripts are in outputs/transcripts/, one JSONL per run. The original Streamlit dashboard is dashboard/app.py.
